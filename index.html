HexOct: The Hexagonality Law

with the Window Law, the Rule of Halves & the Swap Rule

Sixteen papers in one book, from DICTO's PET-bottle-cap DICTOspheres to spin ice and the two HexOct tiles. The Hexagonality Law is a law of structural crystallography: in every close-packed crystal structure, however its layers are stacked, the space is shared out into finitely many kinds of piece, borrowed whole or in half from the cubic and the hexagonal structures; pieces change only by swapping; and every count is a straight line in the structure's hexagonality, the crystallographers' share of hexagonal layers. Then the law in real materials, from hydrogen in titanium to battery cathodes, ice, diamond, nanowires and magnets. Then HexOct: every stacking is a string of just two tiles, and keyed tiles, HexOct-K, can compute. Last, new in Part IV, the Window Law, which contains the Hexagonality Law and marks its exact reach. Version 11: 134 pages with 13 plates and an index.
